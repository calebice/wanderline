# Realism asset prompts

These prompts document approved original assets. Source names and existing artwork
were intentionally excluded from image-generation instructions.

## Current guide reference: naturalistic colored pencil

Use case: stylized-concept

Asset type: reference for an educational drawing-style guide

Create an original, naturalistic full-color landscape drawing of a fictional
Japanese seaside town. Teach observational realism through believable construction,
measured proportions, coherent light, material observation, and atmospheric color.

Show a steep coastal hillside settlement with plausible small Japanese homes,
varied dark ceramic tiled roofs, a narrow weathered stone stair lane descending
toward a modest harbor and open sea, utility poles and connected wires, retaining
walls, garden pots, and dense coastal greenery. The town must be entirely fictional
and must not resemble a famous or recognizable real location.

Use a 3:2 landscape composition and an immersive eye-level view from partway down
the stair lane. Let close foreground surfaces frame a readable descent through the
clustered houses toward the harbor and sea. Use coherent two-point perspective,
natural scale changes, overlaps, and distinct foreground, middle-distance, and
background planes. Keep the architecture and stair as the primary observed subject.

Render the scene as a naturalistic colored-pencil drawing with restrained
wax-pencil layering on subtle warm off-white paper. It must look hand drawn rather
than photographic or wet-watercolor. Preserve fine colored-pencil strokes in roof
tile, worn stone, timber, plaster, vegetation, and water. Model forms through layered
color and value rather than black outlines. Keep sharper edges, richer color, and
material detail near the viewer; simplify and soften distant houses and coast.

Use fresh late-afternoon coastal daylight after light rain, one coherent warm sun
direction, cool reflected sky light in shadows, restrained natural colors, a few
damp stone reflections, and gentle sea haze. Keep the mood calm, ordinary, and
lived-in without fantasy nostalgia or cinematic spectacle.

Create exactly one finished landscape scene. Include no people, characters,
vehicles, readable signage, text, labels, logos, signatures, or watermarks. Keep
architecture structurally coherent and connect roof tiles, windows, stairs, poles,
and wires logically. Avoid copied compositions, iconic landmarks, tourist-postcard
motifs, anime styling, cartoon exaggeration, watercolor blooms, ink outlines,
oil-paint impasto, photographic camera effects, oversaturated color grading,
fantasy lighting, and malformed or repeating buildings.

## Learner reference: simplified naturalistic colored pencil

Use case: stylized-concept

Asset type: attainable beginner-to-intermediate drawing reference

Create a simpler companion study derived from the approved colored-pencil Realism
reference. Preserve its naturalistic colored-pencil character, fictional Japanese
coastal setting, calm daylight, believable construction, and lived-in feeling while
reducing the visual problem so it can be drawn in four clear stages.

Show a short stone stair lane descending toward the sea between two clearly readable
small houses: one dominant house mass and one secondary house mass. Include one low
retaining wall, one utility pole with a few logically connected wires, three or four
grouped foliage masses, a simple distant headland, and broad unobstructed sea and sky.
Suggest only a few distant roof silhouettes. Omit harbor engineering, repeated
rooftops, many poles, many pots, intricate masonry, and tangled vegetation.

Use a 3:2 eye-level landscape composition with the stair as one large directional
shape leading to the water. Organize five major masses: sky, sea/headland, left
house, right house/wall, and stair/foliage foreground. Use a single clear horizon,
straightforward two-point perspective, and large uncluttered negative spaces. The
scene should be constructible from boxes, wedges, and foliage envelopes.

Render naturalistic colored pencil on subtle warm off-white paper with visible
layered strokes, restrained local color, no black contour treatment, and broad quiet
surfaces. Limit finish to roof-tile rhythm, worn stair stone, and grouped leaf edges.
Use no more than four major value groups and reserve the darkest accents for the near
house and stair.

Use one coherent warm late-afternoon sun direction with cool reflected shadow and
simple cast-shadow shapes. Create depth through overlap, scale, softer distant
edges, and muted background color rather than tiny detail.

Create exactly one finished landscape scene. Include no process panels, text,
people, characters, vehicles, readable signage, logos, signatures, or watermarks.
Keep the fictional houses, roof planes, stairs, pole, and wires structurally
plausible. Avoid anime, cartoon, watercolor, ink outlines, photographic effects,
fantasy lighting, visual clutter, and malformed architecture.

## Approved learner process sheet

Use case: infographic

Asset type: attainable four-stage drawing process sheet

Use the approved simplified colored-pencil learner reference as the exact scene and
crop. Create four equal landscape panels in a 2-by-2 grid on warm paper. Preserve
the same houses, stair, wall, pole, headland, sea, sky, and foliage placement in all
panels. Make the sequence cumulative by keeping earlier information faint while the
new decision becomes prominent. Include no embedded captions or tutorial chrome;
the product supplies accessible written checkpoints separately.

Panel one reduces the scene to five broad masses: sky, sea/headland, left house,
right house/wall, and stair/foliage foreground. Panel two ghosts those masses and
adds only horizon, convergence, house boxes, roof wedges, stair rhythm, wall plane,
one pole, and foliage envelopes in restrained construction colors. Panel three
retains the structure lightly and adds four value groups, warm sunlight, cool
shadow, natural color families, and atmospheric distance with no texture. Panel
four adds only roof-tile rhythm, selected stone and stair marks, grouped leaf edges,
and a few window and wire accents.

Keep the transition visibly limited at every stage. Do not introduce new objects or
let early panels become detailed. Include no text, people, characters, vehicles,
signage, logos, signatures, or watermarks. Avoid alternate compositions, dense town
detail, tangled wires, malformed architecture, photographic effects, and decorative
tutorial graphics.

## Future collection reference: observational graphite

Use case: stylized-concept

Asset type: reference reserved for a future graphite/pencil-specific guide

Create an original realistic graphite landscape drawing of a fictional Japanese
seaside town. Teach observational realism through accurate construction, truthful
proportion, believable perspective, close observation, and controlled values.

Show a steep coastal hillside settlement with plausible small Japanese homes,
varied dark ceramic tiled roofs, narrow stone stairs descending through the
buildings, glimpses of a modest harbor and open sea, utility poles and wires,
retaining walls, and dense coastal greenery. The place must be entirely fictional
and not resemble a famous or recognizable real location.

Use a 3:2 landscape composition and an elevated three-quarter view. Let the stair
route connect foreground, clustered midground architecture, and distant water. Use
overlap and clearly differentiated depth planes while keeping perspective
internally consistent.

Render a highly observed graphite drawing on warm white, lightly toothy paper.
Retain light construction, varied line pressure, layered hatching, soft graphite
transitions, reserved paper highlights, and selective dark accents. Describe roof,
masonry, wood, foliage, and water textures realistically, concentrating detail near
the stair and foreground and quieting distant forms. Use a full tonal range without
graphic black outlines.

Use clear late-morning coastal light with one coherent sun direction. Let cast
shadows describe stairs, eaves, walls, and foliage. Recede the sea and far shore
through lighter values, softer edges, and reduced detail. Keep the atmosphere calm,
ordinary, and lived-in.

Create exactly one finished landscape scene. Include no people, characters,
vehicles, readable signage, text, labels, logos, signatures, or watermarks. Keep
architecture plausible and utility connections logical. Avoid copied compositions,
iconic landmarks, tourist-postcard motifs, anime styling, cartoon exaggeration,
watercolor washes, color pigment, photographic effects, and malformed or repeating
buildings.
